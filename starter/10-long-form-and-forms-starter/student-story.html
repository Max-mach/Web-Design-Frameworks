Aoife’s story

Second-year Psychology

I was diagnosed with ADHD at the end of first year, which felt very late until I met about six other people the same term who had the same story. Before that, I had spent a year assuming everyone found deadlines this hard and just complained about it less.

The first thing I did was nothing. I sat on the diagnosis for a month because booking an appointment meant admitting I needed one. What finally moved me was a Tuesday drop-in, no appointment, no form, you just turn up. I told myself I was only going to ask a question.

What changed

The adviser did not lead with accommodations. She asked how I actually worked, and we spent most of the time on the gap between starting a task and being able to start a task. The practical things came out of that conversation rather than from a checklist:

  • Coursework extensions I could request without explaining myself each time.
  • A reader-writer for exams, once we worked out that reading under time pressure was where I lost most marks.
  • Lecture recordings, so missing five minutes did not mean missing the thread for the rest of the hour.
  • The co-working room on Riverside campus, which turned out to matter more than any of the formal accommodations.

The co-working room is just a room with other people working in it. For reasons I have stopped trying to explain, I can work there for three hours and barely notice. On my own I lose the first two hours to my phone.

I thought asking for help meant I was behind. It turned out to be the thing that let me catch up.

If you are putting it off

Go to a drop-in and treat it as reconnaissance. You do not have to decide anything or accept anything. I brought one question and left with a plan I did not know was available, and the whole thing took about twenty minutes.
